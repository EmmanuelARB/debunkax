# The addressee of « tu » / « vous » Claims: a named person, else the last other politician heard, else unknown

« Vous avez voté cette loi » can't be checked without knowing who « vous » is, and the Check came back unverifiable. The addressee is settled in this order: a person the sentence or the context just before names directly (« Monsieur X, vous… »); else, only when the sentence is clearly aimed at someone, the last politician other than the claimant recognised by voice in the 30 s before the sentence (the transcript window), journalists and presenters skipped; else unknown. It is looked up only for a second-person sentence by a recognised Speaker, and given to the provider as « probable ».

ADR 0003 forbids guessing the Speaker from what was said. A name spoken to someone is not that guess: it is evidence about the addressee, not a claim about who is talking, and the Speaker stays voice-only. Journalists are skipped because in a debate the last other voice is often the moderator, while the person a politician accuses is the opponent; pinning a politician's act on the moderator would be a wrong name, the worst error. A lookup that finds nobody in 30 s stays unknown rather than reaching further back: the older the voice, the weaker the link to the « vous ».

## Consequences

- If the Claim depends on an addressee that remains uncertain, the Verdict is unverifiable, never a guess. « vous, les Français » or absentees follow the sentence itself.
- Roles (politician or journalist) are part of the enrolment, so adding a person means stating their role.
- Not measured on live sessions yet.
